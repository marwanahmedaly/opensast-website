Experiment history

The questions changed.
The evidence stayed.

From AST excerpts and autonomous agents to source specifications, reusable suspects, focused evidence and three separately scored evaluations.

01 / July–August · AST excerpts

Repository → AST → LLM

Ranked retrieval overlapped vulnerable regions in 3 of 15 repositories. Exhaustive analysis projected 197,604 contexts; this was not a completed model run. A completed 225-context Picklescan experiment missed its labeled mechanism with both tested interfaces.

Lesson: More code and more requests did not guarantee the necessary evidence or judgment.

02 / August–September · Agents

Tool-assisted repository investigation

Early autonomous runs sometimes stopped before inspecting necessary code. Later bounded vulnerable/fixed pairs worked on Picklescan and Smolagents. A Fory replay completed in about 111.5 minutes, but 63 of 84 slices remained incomplete.

Lesson: Selected successes did not establish complete review or practical general runtime. These earlier scopes differ from the final four-CWE evaluations.

03 / Plans 4 and 6

Follow sources, then confront the scale

LLM-generated specifications, CodeQL flow and a second verification step revealed source, flow and sink gaps. A source inventory found 1,036,311 sites across 48 revisions. Only two of 23 visible known sources appeared in the first 60 positions.

Lesson: Removing a cap exposes the workload; ranking alone does not prove detection.

04 / Plan 7

Build reusable, recall-oriented suspects

The historical 34-rule layer and stock union reached 30 of 31 known development operations. An earlier static-only four-CWE benchmark evaluation recorded 55.3% union recall. This was a different version and protocol from the later complete-pipeline benchmark. The later completed pipeline used 35 custom runnable queries.

Lesson: Candidate coverage depends on the cohort. A suspect is not yet a verified vulnerability.

05 / October 3–4

Compare the complete pipeline

Across 31-project evaluation, the corrected labeled-panel recall increased from 38.1% to 92.9%. Reviewed alert precision fell from 80.0% to 62.0%, reflecting a larger review burden.

Lesson: Broader useful coverage can accompany more false alerts.

06 / Earlier investigation

Establish the seven-project panel

An earlier scan informed subsequent investigation of these repository families. Its records remain archived; the presented seven-project evaluation is therefore development-exposed.

Lesson: Prior exposure must remain disclosed when selecting the presented results.

07 / October 5–7

Diagnose, experiment and freeze

Context and output investigations were followed by prompt revisions, CWE instruction modules and a small QLoRA pilot. Tested replacements did not establish a consistent detection improvement and were rejected. The implementation was frozen for thesis reporting.

Lesson: Preserve unsuccessful experiments and evaluate changes beyond their development examples.

08 / October 8–9

Rescan and benchmark the current pipeline

The 7-project repository evaluation achieved labeled-case precision 80.0%, recall 57.1% and F1 66.7%. On OWASP BenchmarkPython, the pipeline detected 83 of 114 official four-CWE positives, compared with 56 for stock CodeQL. The real-project alert stream remained noisy.

Lesson: Keep historical and current scans separate; these exposed tests are not fresh generalization evidence.

Historical evidence: thesis §§3.1–3.2 and 5.2, with the final evaluations in §§5.3.1–5.3.3. The approaches and scoring units changed over time; historical figures are not pooled with the final scans.

REJECTED ALTERNATIVES

What did not become the final system?

A variant is not promoted because it works on the examples used to develop it. The frozen results retain their original configuration.

Source-first classification

The broad inventory produced an impractical projected inference workload. Materializing batches was not a completed full-model evaluation.

Small QLoRA pilot

54 training/validation examples, including 40 controlled examples. On the laboratory panel of eight positive cases and two emitted safe controls, F1 fell from 66.7% to 46.2%. This score does not cover all 270 evaluated candidates per arm. The adapter was rejected (thesis §5.5.1).

Prompts & CWE modules

Development improvements did not hold consistently in reserved tests. Reduced alerts sometimes accompanied lost true vulnerabilities.

Output repair recovered valid decisions on selected prior failures. It did not retrospectively change the completed full-scan accuracy results.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS / CONTRIBUTIONS

What did the project establish?

An implemented local pipeline, traceable evaluation records, and a measured account of its limits. General production detection remains open.

RQ1 / Suspect coverage
Development gains over stock CodeQL; cohort-dependent coverage and XSS discovery gaps remain.
RQ2 / Verification
Filters false alerts, but also loses true mechanisms. A general advantage in detection, precision and cost together was not established.
RQ3 / Evidence
Missing relationships and misinterpretation both remain. Valid references do not establish semantic correctness.
RQ4 / Adaptation
The tested prompt, CWE instruction and small QLoRA replacements did not establish a superior replacement on their later panels.
RQ5 / Local product
CLI, dashboard, workers and GitHub Actions demonstrate a bounded prototype with conservative verifier reuse.
